Documents to Bring to a Consultation

Prepare enough context for a useful conversation while protecting information that does not need to be transmitted yet.

A consultation is most useful when you can explain the need, identify important dates, and ask focused questions.

Start with the reason for meeting

Write a two- or three-sentence description of what you are trying to accomplish, what has already happened, and what decision or responsibility feels unclear. List any deadlines or upcoming events.

Prepare context by service area

  • Tax: a list of filing types, years involved, document categories available, and known missing items.
  • Insurance: who or what needs protection, current coverage categories, important obligations, and questions about terminology.
  • Business: business activity, ownership, formation status, current systems, records, and immediate priorities.
  • Documents or notary: document name, receiving-party instructions, signer information, and appointment requirements.

Bring a question list

  • What is the appropriate service or starting point?
  • What information will be needed next?
  • What is outside the scope of the service?
  • What timeline or dependency should I understand?
  • How should sensitive documents be provided securely?

Protect sensitive information

Do not send Social Security numbers, full tax returns, identity documents, banking information, insurance records, or confidential business files through a public form or unsecured message. During the initial request, describe the document instead of attaching it. Wait for secure handling instructions.

After the consultation

Record the agreed next step, information requested, responsible person, and follow-up timing. Clarify any scope or instruction you do not understand before sending records or beginning work.
