Confirm the document, identification, signers, and receiving-party instructions before the appointment.

A notary verifies specific facts about a signing. Preparing the document correctly helps avoid preventable delays.

Before the appointment

  • Confirm that notarization is required and identify any instructions from the receiving organization
  • Review the document for blank spaces, missing pages, and obvious incompleteness
  • Confirm which signers must appear and whether witnesses are required
  • Ask what current identification is acceptable
  • Do not sign in advance unless specifically instructed that the signature may be acknowledged

Bring the complete document

Bring every page, including pages without signature lines. The notary cannot choose the form, fill in legal content, or decide which notarization is required for you.

Understand the notary’s role

A notary may verify identity, willingness, awareness, an oath or affirmation, or an acknowledgment as permitted. A notary does not provide legal advice, explain legal consequences, or determine whether a document protects your interests.

If the document is unclear

Pause and contact the document’s issuing or receiving organization. Questions about legal meaning should be directed to a qualified attorney. Do not ask the notary to select language or advise you how to complete a legal form.

Requirements vary by document and jurisdiction. Confirm current appointment instructions before arriving.
